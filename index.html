2. The 10-Can Deposit Pipeline & Direct Player Bounty
Arcade Gameplay to On-Chain Execution Pipeline Instant Player Payout

Step 1: Street Arcade Collection: Player roams the top-down canvas picking up cans. Client holds can inventory up to a capped batch of 10 cans.

Step 2: Deposit at SUV: Player interacts with the red SUV to deposit the 10 cans into the tank.

Step 3: Wallet Sign & Fill: Game prompts wallet connection. Player signs the fillOil() transaction and pays a fraction of a cent in Base network gas. The contract enforces a 10-second global tank cooldown to prevent same-block reentrancy or bot collisions (no per-wallet cooldown in beta).

Step 4: Split Distribution: The Oil Tank contract releases 10 DRB: 9 DRB routes into the Drop Vault for NFT holders/scavengers, while 1 DRB transfers immediately to msg.sender as a direct in-game reward for pumping the tank.

Bot Dynamic: If an automated keeper bot calls fillOil() first, the bot pays the network gas and earns the 1 DRB bounty, while the 9 DRB still drops into the community vault. The game loop never stalls.

Why An On-Chain Captcha Contract Fails Against Bots (And The Working Solution)

The Proposal: A 3-step contract where the browser triggers Call 1, solves a captcha, and triggers Call 2 within a 10-second window to release 10 DRB.

EVM Execution Reality: Smart contracts are public and deterministic. A bot monitoring the mempool or the public blockchain can call both Contract 1 and Contract 2 within the exact same block (or even via a single atomic smart contract transaction) in under 20 milliseconds. Furthermore, on-chain code cannot independently verify whether a web user solved an image captcha in their browser without an off-chain cryptographic signature.

The Real Solution (Option A — Fixed Caller Bounty): Instead of fighting bots with brittle on-chain timers, offer a fixed 1 DRB caller bounty inside fillOil(). MEV searchers will happily pay Base gas 24/7 to pump the tank the instant cooldown resets, completely removing the need for you to run servers or pay gas.

The Real Solution (Option B — EIP-712 Signed Turnstile): If you strictly require human verification before a relayer submits gas, the user solves an off-chain Cloudflare Turnstile in the browser. A lightweight serverless backend verifies the token and signs an EIP-712 permit: (userAddress, nonce, deadline). The relayer submits the transaction only when the signature is valid.
